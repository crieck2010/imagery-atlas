01 / FREE DATA

Ranked by operational value

Resolution × revisit is the primary ranking signal; “best use” breaks the tie. A lower rank can still be the right answer when spectral or temporal needs dominate.

Default stack

Start with Element84 Earth Search or Microsoft Planetary Computer for COG-backed Sentinel-2, Landsat, HLS, Sentinel-1, MODIS, GOES, Copernicus DEM and NAIP. Discovery is open; some pixel reads need a free account or cloud credentials.[1][2]

02 / SOURCE DOSSIERS

What the pixels actually contain

Open a dossier for band geometry, cadence, programmatic access, license posture and the ingestion trap most likely to bite.

Sentinel-2 MSI

ESA / Copernicus
10 m~5 dcommercial use
+
Constellation
S-2A (2015), S-2B (2017), S-2C (2024). Land and coastal coverage from 84°N to 84°S.[3]
GSD
10 m: B2/B3/B4/B8. 20 m: B5/B6/B7/B8A/B11/B12. 60 m: B1/B9. B10 appears in L1C only.
Spectral bands · center wavelength µm
B1 coastal 0.443; B2 blue 0.49; B3 green 0.56; B4 red 0.665; B5/B6/B7 red-edge 0.704/0.740/0.783; B8 NIR 0.842; B8A narrow NIR 0.865; B9 water vapor 0.945; B10 cirrus 1.3735; B11/B12 SWIR 1.61/2.19.
Programmatic access
Earth Search: sentinel-2-c1-l2a, sentinel-2-pre-c1-l2a, sentinel-2-l2a, sentinel-2-l1c. MPC: sentinel-2-l2a. CDSE: sentinel-2-l2a. Discovery is open; CDSE reads need a free account. No verified hard rate limit; fair-use throttling applies.[1][2][4]
License
Copernicus open terms: free worldwide use, including commercial, with attribution.
Ingestion note
Critical: Collection 1 L2A reflectance uses scale 0.0001 and offset −0.1; legacy products use offset 0. Read raster:bands. Earth Search keys are semantic lowercase; MPC keys are uppercase band IDs. CDSE serves original JP2s, not COGs.

Landsat Collection 2

NASA / USGS
15–100 m8 d combinedpublic domain
+
Constellation
Landsat 8 (2013) and 9 (2021), with Collection 2 L2 heritage from Landsat 4/5/7 back to 22 Aug 1982.
GSD & cadence
30 m multispectral; 15 m panchromatic; TIRS native 100 m resampled to 30 m. Each satellite repeats in 16 days; combined cadence is 8 days.
OLI / TIRS bands · center wavelength µm
B1 coastal 0.44; B2 blue 0.48; B3 green 0.56; B4 red 0.65; B5 NIR 0.87; B6/B7 SWIR 1.61/2.20; pan 0.50–0.68; B9 cirrus 1.37; B10/B11 thermal 10.9/12.0.
Programmatic access
Earth Search and MPC: landsat-c2-l2. USGS STAC: landsat-c2l2-sr, -st, landsat-c2ard-*, landsat-c2l1, and L3 -ba, -fsca, -dswe. Use /stac-server; bare /stac returns 403.[5]
License
US federal data; public domain.
Ingestion note
Surface reflectance: scale 0.0000275, offset −0.2. Surface temperature: scale 0.00341802, offset +149 K. Decode qa_pixel bits for cloud, shadow, snow and water. Catalog asset keys differ.

HLS v2.0

NASA IMPACT / LP DAAC
30 m2–3 dpublic domain
+
Virtual constellation
Landsat 8/9 OLI plus Sentinel-2A/B/C MSI, harmonized to a common MGRS grid. Near-global land except Antarctica.[6]
GSD & cadence
30 m; global land every 2–3 days; reported latency about 1.7 days.
Bands
S30 carries 13 Sentinel-derived bands B01–B12 plus B8A, spanning about 0.4439–2.22024 µm, with solar/view angles and Fmask. L30 carries seven OLI bands.
Programmatic access
MPC collections hls2-l30 and hls2-s30; CMR concepts HLSL30.002 and HLSS30.002; LP DAAC direct. MPC also publishes GeoParquet item snapshots.
License
NASA open data; commercial use allowed.
Ingestion note
The default dense time-series cube: common grid and tiling eliminate much harmonization work. Reflectance scale is 0.0001; Fmask is bit-packed. S30 includes Sentinel-2C.

Sentinel-1 SAR

ESA / Copernicus
10 m spacing~6 d designall weather
+
Constellation
C-band SAR. S-1A and S-1C operational in the research snapshot; S-1B failed in 2021. S-1D launched Nov 2025, but commissioning status remained unverified.
Geometry & bands
IW SLC about 5×20 m; GRD typically 10 m pixel spacing with about 20×22 m resolution. C-band 5.405 GHz; VV+VH over land, HH+HV in polar/maritime acquisitions.
Programmatic access
Earth Search sentinel-1-grd: open search, but requester-pays pixels require AWS credentials. MPC sentinel-1-grd and sentinel-1-rtc; RTC requires free PC account SAS signing. CDSE sentinel-1-grd requires account S3 credentials.[1][2][4]
License
Copernicus open terms allow commercial use. MPC’s RTC derivative is CC BY 4.0.
Ingestion note
Prefer terrain-corrected gamma-nought RTC for mapping; it is map-projected float32 with nodata −32768. Use SLC from CDSE or ASF when phase matters for InSAR. Requester-pays GRD on Earth Search can create unexpected cloud charges.

MODIS · Terra / Aqua

NASA
250 m–1 km1–2 dpublic domain
+
GSD & cadence
250 m B1–2; 500 m B3–7; 1 km B8–36. Global coverage every 1–2 days.[7]
Spectrum
36 bands from 0.405 to 14.385 µm: VIS/NIR B1–19, SWIR/MWIR B20–25, LWIR B27–36.
Programmatic access
MPC includes Collection 6.1 products such as modis-13Q1-061 with COGs and source HDF. CMR STAC / LAADS DAAC covers the broader suite. MPC reads require no key; Earthdata token is needed for CMR downloads.
License
Public domain.
Ingestion note
Use daily LST (MOD11A1) and 16-day vegetation index (MOD13Q1; NDVI/EVI scale 0.0001) for long baselines. Terra/Aqua are aging; prefer VIIRS for continuity.

VIIRS

NASA / NOAA
375 / 750 mdailypublic domain
+
Constellation
Suomi-NPP (2011), NOAA-20 (2017), NOAA-21 (2022); daily global coverage across a 3,040 km swath.
GSD & spectrum
375 m for five I-bands; 750 m for 16 M-bands and the Day/Night Band. VIS/NIR, SWIR, MWIR around 3.7 µm, LWIR around 8.5–12 µm; DNB spans 0.5–0.9 µm.
Programmatic access
CMR STAC / LANCE (for example, near-real-time VNP02IMG) and NOAA CLASS. Earthdata token is required for CMR downloads.[8]
License
Public domain.
Ingestion note
I-bands are the sharpest free daily global optical view in this catalog. The DNB supports night-light analysis; VNP14 is the standard rapid active-fire product.

Sentinel-3 OLCI / SLSTR

ESA / EU / EUMETSAT
300 m–1 km~daily–4 dcommercial use
+
GSD & cadence
OLCI 300 m with ~2–4-day global coverage. SLSTR 500 m solar and 1 km thermal, about daily to two-day coverage.
Spectrum
OLCI: 21 bands from 400–1020 nm. SLSTR: solar channels at 0.55/0.65/0.87/1.375/1.61/2.25 µm and thermal channels at 3.7/10.85/12 µm.[9]
Programmatic access
CDSE STAC sentinel-3-* families; free account for reads. EUMETSAT Data Store API uses a free account and API key.
License
Copernicus open terms; commercial use allowed.
Ingestion note
Use for daily ocean color and land/water temperature. Nadir plus oblique SLSTR views complicate raw stacking; prefer L2 composites. OLCI + SLSTR also feed the daily 300 m CLMS Burnt Area products.

GOES-R ABI

NOAA / NASA
0.5–2 km30 sec–10 minpublic domain
+
Cadence & coverage
Mode 6: full disk every 10 minutes, CONUS/PACUS every 5 minutes, mesoscale every 30–60 seconds. Western Hemisphere only.
GSD & bands
0.5 km red B2; 1 km B1/B3/B5; 2 km B4 and B6–B16. 16 channels at 0.47, 0.64, 0.87, 1.38, 1.61, 2.25, 3.89, 6.17, 6.93, 7.34, 8.44, 9.61, 10.33, 11.19, 12.27 and 13.27 µm.[10]
Programmatic access
MPC goes-cmi publishes L2 CMIP/MCMIP as NetCDF and COGs, including Web Mercator. AWS Open Data noaa-goes is public with no key.[11]
License
Public domain.
Ingestion note
GOES uses a geostationary fixed grid, not a normal projected raster. Reproject explicitly before stacking with LEO imagery; use _wm COG assets for quick overlays.

ASTER archive

NASA / METI
15 / 30 / 90 mtaskedpublic domain
+
Instrument
Terra-based, 60 km swath. VNIR: 15 m B1–3 including a backward-looking stereo channel. SWIR: 30 m B4–9. TIR: 90 m B10–14.
Spectrum
VNIR 0.52–0.86 µm; SWIR six bands 1.60–2.43 µm; TIR five bands 8.125–11.65 µm.
Programmatic access
CMR STAC / LP DAAC, including AST_L1T; Earthdata login required for download.[12]
License
Public domain through US distribution.
Ingestion note
Only VNIR remains active. SWIR failed 12 Jan 2009 after anomalies from Apr 2008. TIR was permanently shut down 16 Jan 2026. Version 4 archive reprocessing began in 2025.

NAIP aerial

USDA FSA
0.6 m≥ 3 yrpublic domain
+
Coverage
CONUS aerial surveys; 2010–present in the COG archive. Each state is flown at least every three years.
GSD & bands
Mostly 0.6 m, with some 1 m imagery; 8-bit red, green, blue and NIR.
Programmatic access
Earth Search naip, MPC naip, and the AWS Open Data NAIP bucket. No authentication.
License
Public domain.[13]
Ingestion note
It is aerial, not satellite, and acquisition season / leaf-on conditions vary by state and year. Still the strongest free VHR label and validation source for US projects.

Hyperspectral trio

ASI / DLR / NASA JPL
30–60 m224–285 bandsterms vary
+
PRISMA
ASI; 30 m hyperspectral plus 5 m pan, 30 km swath; 239 bands from 400–2500 nm; ~29-day relook with ±70° pointing.
EnMAP / EMIT
EnMAP: DLR/GFZ, 30 m, 30 km swath, ~224–228 bands from 420–2450 nm. EMIT: NASA JPL on ISS, ~60 m, 75 km swath, ~285 bands from 380–2500 nm.
Programmatic access
PRISMA through ASI portal; EnMAP via DLR EOC GeoService/STAC; EMIT via CMR/LP DAAC. Free registration is typically required.
License
EMIT NASA data are public domain. PRISMA and EnMAP have mission-specific terms; verify commercial permission.
Ingestion note
Use L2D bottom-of-atmosphere products and remove atmospheric-absorption/noise bands. Small, tasked scenes make these spectral R&D sources—not wall-to-wall monitoring feeds.

GEDI lidar

NASA / University of Maryland
25 m footprintssparse trackspublic domain
+
Geometry
ISS-mounted 1064 nm full-waveform lidar. 25 m footprints at 60 m along-track spacing, eight beams, between ±51.6° latitude.
Coverage
Discrete tracks, not a raster. GEDI hibernated 17 Mar 2023–24 Apr 2024 and resumed 26 Apr 2024.
Programmatic access
CMR STAC / LP DAAC, for example GEDI02_A; Earthdata login required.[14]
License
Public domain.
Ingestion note
Join footprint measurements to optical pixels for canopy-height and biomass training or validation. Do not present interpolated footprints as wall-to-wall observed elevation.

ICESat-2 ATLAS

NASA
~17 m footprints91 d repeatpublic domain
+
Geometry
532 nm photon-counting lidar; six beams; ~17 m footprints with ~0.7 m photon sampling along track.
Coverage
Global discrete tracks to ±88° latitude; 91-day repeat.
Programmatic access
CMR STAC / NSIDC collections ATL03, ATL06 and ATL08; Earthdata login required.
License
Public domain.
Ingestion note
Strong free vertical truth for ice and canopy change. ATL08 is the most pipeline-friendly land/vegetation product.

Copernicus DEM

ESA · TanDEM-X / WorldDEM source
30 / 90 mstatic DSMcustom license
+
Products
GLO-30 at ~30 m and GLO-90 at ~90 m; float32 elevation in metres. Near-global, though GLO-30 excludes a small subset of country tiles.
Programmatic access
Earth Search and MPC: cop-dem-glo-30, cop-dem-glo-90. These mirrors avoid requester-pays; the AWS Open Data source bucket is requester-pays.
License
Custom ESA terms, not public domain or CC0. Commercial use is possible with attribution and additional conditions; review the annex for the intended product.
Coverage
Static global baseline; no revisit.
Ingestion note
This is a DSM, so buildings and vegetation are part of the surface. Normalize against GEDI/ICESat-2 or a terrain model such as FABDEM for bare-earth analysis.

SRTM / NASADEM

NASA / USGS
~30 mstaticpublic domain
+
Coverage
Shuttle Radar Topography Mission baseline from 2000, about 60°N–56°S. NASADEM is the reprocessed, void-filled derivative.
GSD
One arc-second, approximately 30 m; single elevation band.
Programmatic access
MPC nasadem, NASA Earthdata and AWS Open Data. MPC requires no authentication for reads.

Planet NICFI mosaics

Planet / Norway NICFI
non-commercial~3.7 muncertain status
+
Coverage & cadence
Monthly PlanetScope tropical-forest mosaics, approximately 3–3.7 m, RGB + NIR; newer SuperDove imagery may carry eight bands.
Programmatic access
Planet Basemaps / Data API with an approved free NICFI account.
License
Non-commercial only, limited to NICFI forest/climate purposes. It cannot be a dependency of a commercial Earthwatch product.

Commercial SAR

~16 cm claim to 3 m · all weather · tasking

04 / BEST-DATA LOGIC

Select by phenomenon, not by headline GSD

Choose a tab for the recommended first source, fallback, and specialist layer. The highest-resolution scene is often not the one that answers the question.

PRIMARY

Sentinel-2 MSI

Red-edge B5–B7 and B8A for stress and species work at 20 m, with 10 m visible/NIR context.

TIME SERIES

HLS v2.0

Use the stack-ready 30 m, 2–3 day record for dense phenology and change features.

SPECIALIST

Hyperspectral

PRISMA, EnMAP, EMIT—or Hyperion archive—for chemistry/species R&D. MODIS VI supports the 2000+ baseline.

PRIMARY

Sentinel-2 MSI

Coastal B1 plus NIR/SWIR water indices at 10–20 m for fine surface-water mapping.

CLOUD / FLOOD

Sentinel-1 RTC

All-weather flood extent. Landsat DSWE L3 is the ready-made historical surface-water layer.

OCEAN

Sentinel-3 OLCI

Daily-scale ocean color and chlorophyll; SLSTR, MODIS or VIIRS for sea-surface temperature.

DETECT / ALERT

VIIRS + GOES

VIIRS/MODIS active fire for global coverage; GOES ABI for minute-scale Western Hemisphere alerts.

SEVERITY

Sentinel-2 / Landsat

Compute dNBR from NIR and SWIR. S1 can map burns through persistent cloud.

READY PRODUCT

CLMS / Landsat L3

Daily 300 m CLMS burned area globally; Landsat C2 L3 BA for the US archive.

PRIMARY

Sentinel-2 MSI

10 m coastal/visible/NIR mapping for shoreline and nearshore condition.

ALL WEATHER

Sentinel-1 RTC

Waterline and shoreline structure through cloud; interpret with tide state rather than assuming tide independence.

CONTEXT

SLSTR + Copernicus DEM

Thermal fronts from SLSTR; GLO-30 supplies a coastal surface-elevation baseline.

DETAIL

Landsat ST

30 m delivered land-surface temperature, derived from 100 m native TIRS.

DAILY

MODIS / VIIRS / SLSTR

Trade spatial detail for daily thermal coverage; SLSTR’s dual view is useful in L2 products.

MINUTES

GOES ABI

Western Hemisphere heat and fire-front evolution at 5–10 minute regional/full-disk cadence.

BASELINE

Copernicus GLO-30

Near-global 30 m DSM. Remember that vegetation and buildings are in the surface.

VERTICAL TRUTH

GEDI / ICESat-2

Sparse footprints for calibration, training and validation—not wall-to-wall change maps.

ACTUAL CHANGE

S-1 SLC InSAR / stereo

Use repeat Sentinel-1 SLC or commercial stereo. Never difference DSMs of unlike provenance without co-registration.

05 / PYTHON INGESTION

A defensible priority order

Centralize catalog differences in one adapter layer. Surface processing metadata rather than burying it in dataset-specific code.

Discover with STAC

Open Earth Search or MPC with pystac_client.Client.open(...). For large AOIs, use MPC GeoParquet item snapshots through adlfs instead of paging the API.

Sign only when needed

Call planetary_computer.sign_inplace(item) for MPC protected assets. Keep AWS requester-pays credentials isolated to the S1 GRD path; prefer free RTC mirrors when phase is not required.

Window COGs, then cube

Use rasterio for single-window range reads; stackstac.stack(...) or odc.stac.load(...) for xarray cubes. Reproject on read to target UTM or an HLS MGRS tile.

Apply metadata-driven scaling

Read each asset’s raster:bands scale and offset. Never hardcode the S2, Landsat reflectance or Landsat temperature transforms.

Normalize QA and keys

S2: SCL or Collection 1 cloud/snow probability. Landsat: qa_pixel bits. HLS: Fmask. Map Earth Search semantic lowercase keys to MPC uppercase IDs in the catalog adapter.

Preserve native evidence

Keep item IDs, collection IDs, processing baseline, scale/offset, CRS and source license with every derived output. Resample 20/60 m S2 only once; reproject GOES explicitly.
